What it shows you

A thousand processes become the sixty apps you actually run, each with a reason attached.

Now

Live readings for CPU, memory, GPU, disk, network, battery and the Neural Engine, refreshed every second while you look. Each one names the app behind it. Any reading can sit in the menu bar on its own.

Apps and history

Every app keeps a record of what it did: hosts it connected to, memory it gained, moments it stopped responding. Vardis keeps that history on your Mac, so you can rewind to the moment the fans spun up and see why.

Disk Cleanup

Finds what developers leave behind: Xcode DerivedData, old simulators, stale node_modules, Docker leftovers and package caches. Each item says how big it is, when it was last used, and whether it rebuilds. Everything goes to the Trash, so nothing is lost until you decide.

Claude Code

Vardis finds your Claude Code sessions on its own. See tokens, cost and how much of your plan is left, search every past session, and pick one up again in your terminal. Several accounts sit side by side.

Fast by design

A monitor should never be the thing slowing your Mac down. Native Swift, no Electron, no web views.

  • < 50 msto open the window
  • < 16 msto switch screens
  • < 0.1%CPU while idle
  • < 40 MBmemory while idle

These are release gates, measured on an M1 with 8 GB of memory. A build that misses one does not ship.

Vardis collects nothing.

No account, no analytics, no crash reports. There is no Vardis server, so there is nowhere for your data to go. Everything it reads and remembers stays on your Mac. It goes online for two things only.

  • Checking for updatesOnly in the direct download, and you can turn it off.
  • Your Claude plan limitsAsked of Anthropic with your own sign-in, sent only to Anthropic.

Questions

Is it really free?

Yes, free forever. There is no paid tier, no trial and no account. Vardis is open source under the MIT License.

What does it collect?

Nothing. There is no analytics, no telemetry and no crash reporting. Vardis only goes online to check for updates, which you can turn off, and to ask Anthropic for your own Claude plan limits. The privacy page has the details.

Should I get the App Store version or the direct download?

Both come from the same code. The App Store version runs in Apple's sandbox, so it cleans only folders you pick, reads Claude Code after you choose its folder once, and cannot switch between Claude accounts or show system processes and sensors. The direct download does all of that and updates itself.

Does it run on Apple silicon and Intel Macs?

Yes. Vardis is a native app for both, on macOS 15 or later.

Is it open source?

Yes. The code is on GitHub under the MIT License. You can read it, build it yourself and send changes. The Vardis name and logo are not covered by the license.
